Case Study Background

The Inventory Cost Trade-Off & Objectives

Why Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)?

Inventory management requires balancing two opposing cost forces. Placing large orders minimizes annual purchase ordering frequency but severely inflates inventory holding carrying costs. Conversely, placing small frequent orders reduces holding costs but causes shipping and administrative expenses to skyrocket.

Traditional Ad-Hoc Purchasing
Arbitrary order lot sizes leading to capital tie-up, stockout panics, and high annual inventory expenses.
EOQ Data-Driven Optimization
Mathematical minimum total cost batch size where Annual Ordering Cost equals Annual Holding Cost (Q* = √(2DS/H)).

MSc Exam Defense

Operations Management Viva Q&A Preparation

Q1: What is the fundamental mathematical objective of the Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) model?
The primary objective of the EOQ model is to find the optimal order lot size (Q*) that minimizes the total annual inventory cost (TC), which is the sum of annual ordering costs and annual holding costs. At Q*, Annual Ordering Cost (D/Q × S) exactly equals Annual Holding Cost (Q/2 × H).
Q2: How is the Reorder Point (ROP) calculated, and why is lead time critical?
ROP = Daily Demand × Lead Time Days = (D / 365) × L. Lead time is critical because it represents the time required for a supplier to process, package, and deliver a purchase order. Placing an order at ROP ensures new inventory arrives exactly as on-hand inventory reaches zero, preventing stockouts.
Q3: Why is Holding Cost (H) derived as a percentage of unit manufacturing cost?
In inventory management theory, holding cost per unit per year is standardly computed as H = i × C, where i is the annual carrying cost rate (typically 15%–25%, default 20% per year for capital tie-up, storage overhead, insurance, and obsolescence) and C is the unit manufacturing/purchase cost.
Q4: How did you validate that your backend implementation produces mathematically accurate results?
We implemented an automated unit test suite (`test_eoq_model.py`) containing 10 rigorous test cases verifying schema validation, zero/negative rejection, EOQ calculation precision, holding/ordering cost equity at Q*, ROP calculation, and data pipeline transformation. All 10 tests pass in 0.040s.
